# 1.3 - Mobile Device Networks

## Main Idea

Mobile devices use several different network technologies to communicate and provide services such as voice calls, cellular data, Wi-Fi connectivity, Bluetooth connections, device tethering, and location services.

This lesson covers cellular generations, Wi-Fi, hotspot functionality, SIM/eSIM, Bluetooth pairing, and GPS/location technologies.

## Important Concepts

-   Cellular networks and cells
-   3G, 4G/LTE, LTE Advanced, and 5G
-   802.11 Wi-Fi
-   Wi-Fi calling
-   Mobile hotspot
-   SIM and eSIM
-   Multiple SIMs
-   Bluetooth pairing
-   Bluetooth PINs
-   GPS
-   Wi-Fi and cellular-based location

## Detailed Explanation

### Cellular Networks

A **cellular network** divides a geographic area into smaller areas called **cells**.

Each cell is served by antennas that provide wireless coverage using specific radio frequencies. As a mobile device moves between cells, the cellular network can maintain communication by connecting the device to different cellular antennas.

Cellular networks provide:

-   Voice communication
-   Cellular data
-   Internet access
-   Other mobile services

The user can control how the phone uses these cellular capabilities. For example, **cellular data** can be disabled while voice communication remains available.

### Airplane Mode

**Airplane mode** disables the device's wireless communication functions.

After enabling airplane mode, the user can usually manually turn specific wireless technologies back on, depending on the device and operating system.

For example, a phone can be placed in airplane mode and then have Wi-Fi enabled again.

---

### Cellular Generations

Cellular technology has evolved through several generations.

#### 3G

**3G** significantly increased the amount of data that could be transmitted over cellular networks.

This enabled mobile devices to support services such as:

-   GPS functionality
-   Mobile television
-   Video on demand
-   Video conferencing
-   Other data-intensive services

#### 4G / LTE

**4G** introduced much higher data capabilities than 3G.

**LTE (Long-Term Evolution)** is commonly associated with 4G cellular networks.

The transcript describes 4G/LTE bandwidths of up to approximately **150 Mbps**.

You may also encounter **GSM (Global System for Mobile Communications)** and **EDGE (Enhanced Data Rates for GSM Evolution)** when discussing cellular technologies.

#### LTE Advanced

**LTE Advanced (LTE-A)** further increased cellular throughput.

The transcript gives a maximum throughput of approximately **300 Mbps**.

#### 5G

**5G (fifth-generation cellular networking)** provides significantly higher bandwidth than previous generations.

Depending on the frequency and implementation, the transcript describes:

-   Up to **10 Gbps** over higher-frequency 5G
-   Approximately **100–900 Mbps** over other 5G frequencies

The increased bandwidth allows mobile devices and **IoT (Internet of Things)** devices to transmit much larger amounts of data.

For example, an IoT device can quickly send information to cloud services for processing.

---

### 802.11 Wi-Fi

**802.11** refers to the family of wireless networking standards commonly known as **Wi-Fi**.

Unlike cellular networks, Wi-Fi generally provides connectivity over a more limited local area.

A typical Wi-Fi network uses an **access point (AP)** that mobile devices connect to.

Wi-Fi can provide:

-   Internet access
-   Voice communication
-   Video
-   General data transfer
-   Local network connectivity

Mobile phones can also use Wi-Fi for voice calls.

### Wi-Fi Calling

**Wi-Fi calling** allows a mobile phone to send and receive voice calls over a Wi-Fi network instead of relying directly on the cellular radio network.

This is useful when cellular coverage is weak but a reliable Wi-Fi connection is available.

For example:

```
Phone
  │
  │ Wi-Fi
  ▼
Access Point
  │
  ▼
Internet / Carrier
```

The phone can still make and receive calls even when the 4G/5G signal is poor, assuming the carrier and device support Wi-Fi calling.

---

### Mobile Hotspot

A smartphone can function as a small wireless router using **hotspot functionality**.

The phone connects to the cellular network and then shares that Internet connection with other devices, commonly through Wi-Fi.

Example:

```
          Cellular Network
                 │
                5G
                 │
              Phone
                 │
              Wi-Fi
        ┌────────┼────────┐
        ▼        ▼        ▼
      Laptop    Tablet   Other Device
```

The phone acts as the connection between the cellular network and the other devices.

Hotspot functionality is commonly built into modern mobile operating systems, but the **mobile carrier may need to allow it**. Some carriers may also charge extra for hotspot usage.

### SIM

**SIM (Subscriber Identity Module)** identifies a subscriber and allows the mobile network to associate the device with a particular cellular service.

A traditional SIM is a **physical card** inserted into the phone.

It contains information related to:

-   The subscriber
-   The carrier/network
-   Subscriber identity
-   SIM identification information

A physical SIM can be removed from one phone and inserted into another compatible phone.

This allows the cellular service associated with that SIM to be moved to the other device.

The transcript also describes SIM storage for contacts and messages.

---

### eSIM

An **eSIM (embedded SIM)** provides SIM functionality without requiring a removable physical SIM card.

The eSIM is built into the device and cannot simply be removed and inserted into another phone.

Instead, the cellular profile can be configured or transferred through software.

An eSIM can commonly be added or transferred using methods such as:

-   Device-to-device transfer
-   Carrier activation
-   QR code

This makes switching or managing cellular profiles easier.

### Multiple SIMs

Some phones support multiple SIM profiles simultaneously.

This allows one phone to have multiple:

-   Phone numbers
-   Cellular profiles
-   Carrier connections

This can be useful when separating personal and work numbers or using different cellular services.

---

### Bluetooth Pairing

Before two Bluetooth devices can communicate, they usually need to go through a process called **pairing**.

Examples include:

-   Smartphone + Bluetooth headset
-   Smartphone + wireless speaker
-   Smartphone + keyboard

Pairing establishes a relationship between the devices and provides a security mechanism for confirming that the correct devices are connecting.

Once two devices are paired, they can normally reconnect automatically later without repeating the complete pairing process.

#### Bluetooth Pairing Process

A typical pairing process is:

1.  Enable Bluetooth on both devices.
2.  Put the Bluetooth device into **discoverable/pairing mode**.
3.  Search for available Bluetooth devices.
4.  Select the desired device.
5.  Confirm or enter the required **PIN**.
6.  Complete the pairing process.
7.  Test the connection.

The exact process can vary between devices.

### Bluetooth PIN

A **PIN (Personal Identification Number)** may be required during Bluetooth pairing.

Depending on the device, the PIN can be:

-   A fixed PIN built into the device
-   A dynamically generated PIN

The correct procedure or PIN is normally provided by the device documentation.

After successful pairing, the devices can communicate and reconnect automatically in the future.

---

### GPS

**GPS (Global Positioning System)** is a satellite-based positioning system originally created by the **United States Department of Defense**.

GPS uses signals from satellites orbiting Earth to determine a device's location.

The transcript states that receiving signals from **four satellites** provides accurate GPS readings.

GPS can determine information such as:

-   **Latitude**
-   **Longitude**
-   **Altitude**

For example, navigation and mapping applications can use GPS to determine the phone's location.

### Other Location Technologies

GPS is not the only technology that can determine a device's approximate location.

#### Wi-Fi-Based Location

A device can use nearby Wi-Fi networks to estimate its location.

This can be useful when GPS is unavailable or has a weak signal.

#### Cellular Triangulation

Cellular networks can also estimate a device's location by using information from multiple cellular towers.

Therefore, a mobile device may still be able to determine an approximate location when GPS reception is unavailable.

-   **Hotspot:** Smartphone shares its cellular Internet connection with other devices, commonly through Wi-Fi.

-   **Bluetooth pairing:** Initial process used to establish a trusted connection between Bluetooth devices.
-   **Bluetooth PIN:** May be required to confirm pairing.
-   **GPS:** Satellite-based positioning system.
-   GPS can determine **latitude, longitude, and altitude**.
-   Other location methods include **Wi-Fi-based positioning** and **cellular tower triangulation**.
