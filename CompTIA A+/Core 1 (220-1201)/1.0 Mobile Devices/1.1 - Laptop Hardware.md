# 1.1 - Laptop Hardware

## Main Idea

Laptops integrate many computer components into a small, portable form factor. This compact design makes laptops convenient but can also make **repair, replacement, and troubleshooting** more difficult than on desktop computers.

Laptop components may be either **modular and replaceable** or **soldered directly to the motherboard**, depending on the manufacturer and model. Important laptop hardware includes batteries, keyboards, memory, storage, wireless adapters, biometric devices, NFC, cameras, and microphones. Pasted text

## Important Concepts

-   Laptop hardware has a smaller **form factor** than desktop hardware.
-   Laptop components vary significantly between manufacturers and models.
-   Batteries may be:
    -   **Modular/removable**
    -   **Built-in**
-   Common laptop battery technologies:
    -   **Lithium-ion (Li-ion)**
    -   **Lithium-ion polymer (Li-Po)**
-   Laptop keyboards commonly connect through a **ribbon cable**.
-   Laptop memory commonly uses **SO-DIMM** modules.
-   Laptop storage can include:
    -   `2.5-inch` hard drives
    -   `2.5-inch` SSDs
    -   **M.2 SSDs**
-   Wireless connectivity may use:
    -   **802.11 / Wi-Fi**
    -   **Bluetooth**
-   **Biometrics** can authenticate users using fingerprints or facial recognition.
-   **NFC** provides very short-range wireless communication.
-   Laptops commonly include built-in **webcams and microphones**.

## Detailed Explanation

### Laptop Form Factor and Modularity

A laptop packs a large amount of technology into a small physical space. This means manufacturers have to design components specifically for the limited space available.

Some laptops are designed to make upgrades and repairs relatively easy. Others make access to internal components difficult.

The amount of **modularity** depends on the laptop model.

For example:

-   A laptop may have replaceable RAM.
-   Another laptop may have RAM soldered directly to the motherboard.
-   One laptop may have a removable battery.
-   Another may have a battery that requires disassembling the laptop to replace.

Therefore, troubleshooting and repair procedures can differ considerably between laptop models. Pasted text

### Laptop Batteries

Two common battery technologies found in laptops are:

-   **Lithium-ion (Li-ion)**
-   **Lithium-ion polymer (Li-Po)**

Both do not have the traditional **memory effect** associated with older battery technologies.

This means you can recharge these batteries without needing to completely discharge them first to preserve their capacity. Pasted text

#### Modular Battery

A modular battery can be removed and replaced relatively easily.

Typically:

1.  Release the battery using a mechanism such as a button or latch.
2.  Remove the existing battery.
3.  Insert the replacement battery.

#### Built-in Battery

A built-in battery is installed inside the laptop.

Replacing it may require:

1.  Opening the laptop.
2.  Disconnecting the battery.
3.  Removing internal components or covers as necessary.
4.  Installing the replacement.

The exact procedure depends on the laptop model.

**Important:** Laptop batteries are not universal. The replacement battery must be compatible with the specific laptop model and form factor. Pasted text

### Laptop Keyboard

The keyboard is one of the most frequently used components on a laptop and may eventually need replacement.

Many laptop keyboards can be removed by:

-   Removing a bezel.
-   Removing screws.
-   Disconnecting the keyboard from the motherboard.

The keyboard commonly connects to the system board through a **ribbon cable**.

#### Troubleshooting a Laptop Keyboard

An external **USB keyboard** can be useful for troubleshooting.

For example:

-   The built-in keyboard does not work.
-   Connect an external USB keyboard.
-   If the external keyboard works, the problem may be related to the laptop's built-in keyboard or its connection.
-   If the external keyboard also has problems, the issue may be related to the operating system or another part of the system.

This provides a way to distinguish between a **hardware problem with the laptop keyboard** and a possible **software/OS problem**. Pasted text

### Laptop Keyboard Features

Laptop keyboards are designed to save space.

They may include:

-   **Windows keys**
-   **Function keys**
-   Secondary functions accessed using key combinations
-   An integrated **numeric keypad** on larger laptops

Individual **keycaps** can sometimes be replaced, but laptop keys are generally more fragile than desktop keyboard keys.

Care must be taken when removing or replacing them because the mechanisms underneath the keycaps can easily be damaged. Pasted text

### Laptop Memory — SO-DIMM

Laptop memory uses a smaller physical form factor than typical desktop memory.

The common laptop memory module is called:

**SO-DIMM — Small Outline Dual In-line Memory Module**

SO-DIMM modules are designed specifically for smaller devices such as laptops.

If the laptop supports modular memory:

1.  Open the appropriate panel.
2.  Remove the existing SO-DIMM if necessary.
3.  Insert the new module into the memory slot.
4.  Push the module down.
5.  Clips on the motherboard lock it into position.

However, some laptops have memory **soldered directly to the motherboard**.

In that situation, the RAM cannot simply be removed and replaced. Upgrading the memory may require replacing the entire system board. Pasted text

---

### Laptop Storage

Laptops need storage for:

-   The operating system
-   Applications
-   User documents
-   Other files

Older laptops commonly used **spinning hard disk drives (HDDs)**.

A desktop HDD commonly uses a `3.5-inch` form factor, while many laptops historically used a smaller:

**2.5-inch drive**

Modern laptops have increasingly moved toward **SSDs**.

### SSD

An **SSD (Solid State Drive)** uses memory to store data and has **no moving parts**.

Advantages include:

-   Faster data access
-   Better resistance to mechanical failure
-   Smaller physical form factors

Older laptops may contain a `2.5-inch SSD`, while newer laptops commonly use an **M.2** form factor. Pasted text

### 2.5-inch vs. M.2

A `2.5-inch` SATA drive traditionally uses two separate connections:

-   **Data**
-   **Power**

An **M.2** drive uses a much smaller form factor and connects directly to an M.2 slot on the motherboard.

A typical M.2 installation involves:

1.  Insert the M.2 drive into the slot at an angle.
2.  Push the drive down.
3.  Secure it with a screw.

M.2 is particularly useful in laptops because of its small physical size. Pasted text

> **Exam focus:**`2.5-inch` describes a drive form factor, while **M.2** describes a smaller physical form factor/interface used by modern laptop storage. M.2 drives can use different underlying storage interfaces, so "M.2" by itself does not mean a specific speed or protocol.

---

### Upgrading an HDD to an SSD

Replacing an older laptop HDD with an SSD can significantly improve the laptop's performance.

Instead of manually:

-   Installing the OS
-   Moving documents
-   Reinstalling applications

you can use **imaging/cloning software**.

### Imaging and Cloning

**Cloning** creates a copy of the contents of one storage drive onto another drive.

For example:

```
Old HDD
   ↓
Cloning / Imaging Software
   ↓
New SSD
```

This can transfer the existing operating system, applications, configuration, and user data to the new drive.

Some SSD manufacturers provide cloning/imaging software with their drives.

Another approach is to create an **image** of the original system on separate storage and then restore that image onto the new SSD.

If both drives can be connected simultaneously, the cloning process can often copy directly from the old drive to the new drive. Pasted text

### Wireless Connectivity

Many modern laptops do not have a dedicated wired Ethernet connection.

Instead, they commonly use wireless technologies such as:

-   **802.11 / Wi-Fi**
-   **Bluetooth**

Wireless functionality may be integrated directly into the motherboard.

Older laptops may use separate modular wireless cards.

### Laptop Wi-Fi Cards

Older laptop wireless adapters may use interfaces such as:

-   **Mini PCI**
-   **Mini PCI Express**

These cards can be removed and replaced on laptops that support this modular design.

Wireless cards connect to antennas installed inside the laptop. Pasted text

### Wireless Antennas

Laptop wireless antennas are commonly positioned around the laptop display.

This places the antennas relatively high when the display is opened.

A Wi-Fi adapter may have:

-   **Main antenna connector**
-   **Auxiliary antenna connector**

Some systems may also have another antenna associated with Bluetooth.

When replacing a wireless card, the antenna wires must be connected correctly to the appropriate connectors. Pasted text

---

### Bluetooth

**Bluetooth** provides short-range wireless communication.

It is commonly used for connecting peripherals such as:

-   Wireless mouse
-   Wireless keyboard
-   Other Bluetooth devices

Bluetooth is associated with a:

**PAN — Personal Area Network**

Modern laptops commonly have Bluetooth integrated directly into the system board rather than using a separate card. Pasted text

---

### Biometric Authentication

Modern operating systems can authenticate users using **biometrics** instead of relying only on passwords.

Common biometric methods include:

-   **Fingerprint recognition**
-   **Facial recognition**

A laptop needs both:

1.  Operating system support for the biometric feature.
2.  Appropriate biometric hardware.

Examples of required hardware include:

-   Fingerprint reader
-   Compatible webcam/camera for facial recognition

### Windows Hello

Windows provides biometric authentication through **Windows Hello**.

Two examples are:

-   **Windows Hello Face** — facial recognition
-   **Windows Hello Fingerprint** — fingerprint authentication

Biometric authentication provides an additional authentication method for securing access to a laptop. Pasted text

---

### NFC

**NFC — Near Field Communication** is a very short-range wireless communication technology.

It generally requires devices to be extremely close to each other, often around:

**4 cm or less**

A common example is using a smartphone or smartwatch to make a contactless payment at a point-of-sale terminal.

NFC is not limited to payments.

It can also be used for authentication in environments such as:

-   Hospitals
-   Warehouses
-   Manufacturing
-   Shipping and receiving

For example, a worker can use an NFC access card or device to authenticate to a workstation. Pasted text

---

### Laptop Camera and Microphone

Most modern laptops include a built-in **webcam**, usually positioned at the top center of the display.

The camera can be used for:

-   Recording video
-   Video conferencing
-   Facial recognition

Laptops commonly also include microphones near the camera.

An **external webcam** can be connected when the user needs:

-   Higher image quality
-   Additional functionality
-   A different camera position

## Exam Notes

-   **Li-ion** and **Li-Po** are common laptop battery technologies.
-   Modern lithium batteries do not have the traditional **memory effect** associated with older battery technologies.
-   Laptop batteries can be **modular** or **built-in**.
-   Battery form factors are **model-specific**; batteries are not universally interchangeable.
-   **SO-DIMM** = Small Outline Dual In-line Memory Module.
-   SO-DIMM is commonly associated with **laptop RAM**.
-   Some laptop RAM is **soldered** to the motherboard and cannot be replaced as a normal RAM module.
-   Older laptop HDDs commonly use the **2.5-inch** form factor.
-   Desktop HDDs commonly use the **3.5-inch** form factor.
-   **SSD** = Solid State Drive.
-   SSDs have **no moving parts**.
-   Older laptops may use `2.5-inch` SSDs.
-   Modern laptops commonly use **M.2** storage.
-   A traditional `2.5-inch` SATA drive has separate **data and power** connections.
-   **Cloning/imaging software** can copy an existing installation from an old drive to a new drive.
-   **802.11** refers to the family of Wi-Fi standards.
-   Older laptops may use **Mini PCI / Mini PCI Express** wireless cards.
-   Laptop wireless antennas are commonly located around the **display**.
-   Wi-Fi cards may have **main** and **auxiliary** antenna connectors.
-   **Bluetooth** is a short-range wireless technology commonly used for peripherals.
-   Bluetooth is associated with a **PAN (Personal Area Network)**.
-   **Biometrics** include fingerprint and facial recognition.
-   **Windows Hello Face** uses facial recognition.
-   **Windows Hello Fingerprint** uses a fingerprint reader.
-   **NFC** = Near Field Communication.
-   NFC is designed for **very short-range communication**, often around `4 cm` or less.
-   NFC can be used for **contactless payments and authentication**.
-   Laptop webcams are commonly located at the **top of the display**.
-   Built-in microphones are commonly located near the webcam.
